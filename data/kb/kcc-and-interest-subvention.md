# Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Interest Subvention on Crop Loans

This note explains the Kisan Credit Card and the government's interest subvention (interest support) on short-term crop loans in simple words.

## What is a Kisan Credit Card?

A **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)** is a credit facility from a bank or cooperative that gives farmers timely loans for farming. Instead of applying for a new loan every season, the farmer gets a credit limit and can withdraw money when needed, up to that limit. Many KCCs come with a **RuPay debit card** so money can be withdrawn at ATMs or used at shops.

## What can a KCC be used for?

A KCC can be used for:

- crop cultivation costs, such as seeds, fertiliser, pesticides, labour and irrigation;
- post-harvest expenses;
- marketing the produce;
- household consumption needs of the farm family, within limits;
- working capital for farm assets and allied activities;
- **animal husbandry** (for example dairy, poultry) and **fisheries**. KCC was extended to animal husbandry and fisheries farmers in 2018-19.

## Who can get a Kisan Credit Card?

Farmers who cultivate land, whether as owners, **tenant farmers**, **oral lessees** or **sharecroppers**, can get a KCC. Self-help groups and joint liability groups of farmers can also get one. Farmers doing animal husbandry or fisheries can get a KCC for those activities.

## Where can I get a KCC?

You can apply at:

- commercial banks;
- Regional Rural Banks (RRBs);
- small finance banks;
- **cooperative banks**, and through your **Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS)**, which gives crop loans to its members with funds from the District Central Cooperative Bank.

## How do I apply for a KCC?

1. Visit your bank branch or PACS and ask for the KCC application form. A simple common form is also available on the PM-KISAN portal.
2. Fill in the form and attach identity proof (for example Aadhaar), address proof, a photograph, and land records or a tenancy or cultivation certificate.
3. The bank or PACS works out your credit limit based on the crops you grow, the area, and the **scale of finance** fixed for your district.
4. After sanction you receive the KCC, and often a RuPay card.

A KCC is typically valid for **five years**, with the limit reviewed every year.

## What is interest subvention on crop loans?

Under the **Modified Interest Subvention Scheme (MISS)** of the Government of India, farmers can get **short-term crop loans up to ₹3 lakh** through KCC at a concessional interest rate of **7% per year**. The government compensates the lending bank for the lower rate.

## What is the prompt repayment incentive?

If you **repay your crop loan on time**, you get an extra **3% prompt repayment incentive (PRI)**. So the **effective interest rate becomes 4% per year** for timely repayment.

| Situation | Interest rate on crop loans up to ₹3 lakh |
| --- | --- |
| Normal concessional rate | **7%** per year |
| Prompt repayment incentive | **3%** |
| Effective rate if repaid on time | **4%** per year |

The loan limits eligible for interest subvention have been revised in the past. The Union Budget 2025-26 announced raising the limit for loans taken through KCC. Ask your bank or PACS for the limit that applies today.

## Does interest subvention apply to animal husbandry and fisheries?

Yes, interest subvention is also available on short-term loans for animal husbandry and fisheries activities under KCC, within the overall limits of the scheme. The sub-limits for these activities are set in the scheme guidelines. Ask your bank for the current figures.

## What happens if I do not repay on time?

If you do not repay by the due date:

- you lose the 3% prompt repayment incentive, so you pay the higher rate;
- after the due date, the bank may charge its normal (higher) interest rate;
- your loan may become overdue, which can affect your chance of getting fresh loans.

The due date is fixed by the bank or PACS, generally based on the harvest and marketing period of your crop. Always ask for the due date in writing.

## Is crop insurance linked with KCC?

Farmers with a KCC or crop loan can take crop insurance under **PMFBY**. Since kharif 2020, PMFBY is **voluntary** for all farmers. If you do not want the bank to enrol you, give a written request to the bank before the season's cut-off date. See the Sahayak note on PMFBY.

## Do I need to give collateral or security for a KCC?

For smaller KCC limits, banks do not ask for collateral security. The collateral-free limit is set by the Reserve Bank of India and has been raised over time. For higher limits, the bank may ask for a mortgage of land or other security. Ask your bank for the current rule.

## Tips for using your KCC wisely

- Borrow only what you need for the season. Interest is charged only on the amount withdrawn and for the period it is used.
- Repay on time to get the 4% effective rate and keep your limit active.
- Keep your passbook or statement updated and check every entry.
- Never share your KCC or RuPay card **PIN** or any **OTP** with anyone, including people who say they are bank staff.
- If anyone asks for a bribe or "commission" to sanction your KCC, complain to the bank's senior officers or through the grievance channels.

## Key points to remember

- KCC gives farmers a flexible credit limit for farming, animal husbandry and fisheries.
- Under MISS: 7% interest on short-term crop loans up to ₹3 lakh, plus a 3% prompt repayment incentive, giving an effective 4%.
- Limits and rules change over time, so confirm them with your bank, PACS or the official sources.
