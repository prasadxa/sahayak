# Financial Literacy and Fraud Safety

This note gives simple money tips for farmers and cooperative members, and explains how to stay safe from banking and online fraud.

## How can I plan my household money better?

- **Write down income and expenses.** Note what money comes in (crop sales, milk, wages) and what goes out every month.
- **Plan for the season.** Farm income often comes in once or twice a year, so set aside money for the months before the next harvest.
- **Save first.** Keep aside a small amount as soon as you receive money, before spending.
- **Build an emergency fund.** Try to keep some savings for illness, crop failure or other emergencies, so you do not have to take costly loans.
- **Avoid unnecessary debt.** Borrow for productive needs, such as farming, and only as much as you can repay.

## Where should I keep my savings?

Keep savings in a **bank account**, a **post office** account, or a **cooperative bank** registered and regulated by law. Every family member can open a basic savings account, for example under the **Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana**, which offers zero-balance accounts.

Bank deposits, including deposits in cooperative banks, are insured by the **Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC)** up to **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank**. Deposits with a **Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS)** are not covered by DICGC, because PACS are not banks.

## Which government schemes give low-cost insurance and pension?

- **Pradhan Mantri Jeevan Jyoti Bima Yojana (PMJJBY):** low-cost life insurance through your bank account.
- **Pradhan Mantri Suraksha Bima Yojana (PMSBY):** low-cost accident insurance through your bank account.
- **Atal Pension Yojana (APY):** a pension scheme for workers in the unorganised sector.
- **PMFBY:** crop insurance. See the Sahayak note on PMFBY.

Premiums, age limits and benefits are revised from time to time, so ask your bank for the current details.

## How can I borrow safely?

- Borrow from **banks, cooperative banks, PACS** or other **regulated lenders**, not from unregistered moneylenders who charge very high interest.
- For crop loans, use a **Kisan Credit Card**. On-time repayment can bring the effective interest rate down to 4% on eligible crop loans. See the Sahayak note on KCC.
- Before signing, ask for the **interest rate, fees, repayment dates and penalties** in writing.
- Never sign blank papers or give your passbook, cheque book or card to an agent.
- Be careful with **loan apps**. Use only apps of banks and lenders that you can verify. Fake loan apps may charge huge fees and misuse your contacts and photos.

## What are the golden rules to avoid fraud?

1. **Never share your OTP, PIN, CVV, password or card number** with anyone. This includes people who say they are from your bank, the RBI, the police or a government office. Real bank staff will never ask for these.
2. **You never need to enter your UPI PIN to receive money.** If someone asks you to enter your PIN or scan a QR code "to receive money", it is a fraud.
3. **Do not click on unknown links** in SMS, WhatsApp or email, especially ones about KYC updates, lottery prizes, electricity disconnection or blocked accounts.
4. **Do not install apps** that someone asks you to download over a phone call, especially screen-sharing or remote-access apps.
5. **Do not trust very high returns.** Schemes promising to double your money quickly, or chit funds and deposit schemes that are not registered, are often frauds.
6. **Check before you pay.** Confirm the name of the person or shop on your UPI app before paying.
7. **Keep your mobile number updated with your bank** so you get SMS alerts for every transaction.

## What are common frauds in villages?

- **Fake KYC calls:** "Your account will be blocked, update KYC now." The caller asks for an OTP or sends a link.
- **Fake scheme or subsidy offers:** "Pay a registration fee to get a government scheme benefit or subsidy." Government schemes do not ask for payment to a person's phone or account.
- **Lottery or prize messages:** "You have won, pay tax or a fee to claim it."
- **Fake job or loan offers** that ask for an advance fee.
- **Ponzi or chain schemes** that pay early members from new members' money until they collapse.
- **Misuse of Aadhaar-based payment services** when fingerprints are taken without clear consent.

## What should I do if I have been cheated online?

Act **immediately**. The faster you report, the better the chance of stopping the money.

1. Call the **National Cyber Crime Helpline 1930** right away.
2. Register a complaint on the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal: cybercrime.gov.in**.
3. **Inform your bank** at once through its customer care number or branch, and ask it to block your card, UPI or account if needed.
4. Keep proof: screenshots, transaction IDs, the fraudster's phone number, messages and dates.
5. You can also file a complaint at your nearest police station.

Under RBI rules, if money is taken from your account through an **unauthorised electronic transaction** that was not your fault, and you **report it to your bank quickly** (within three working days), your liability is generally **zero**. Delays can increase your liability, so report at once.

## What if my bank does not solve my complaint?

First complain to your bank in writing and keep the complaint number. If the bank does not reply within 30 days, or you are not satisfied with the reply, you can complain to the **RBI Ombudsman** through the **RBI Complaint Management System (cms.rbi.org.in)**. Which banks and cooperative banks are covered depends on their type, so check the portal.

For complaints about a **PACS** or a cooperative society, go to the society first and then to the **Registrar of Cooperative Societies**. See the Sahayak note on grievance redressal.

## How can I report an unregistered deposit or chit scheme?

You can report entities that collect deposits illegally or promise unrealistic returns on the RBI's **Sachet portal (sachet.rbi.org.in)**. Taking deposits through unregulated schemes is banned by law.

## Key points to remember

- Never share your OTP, PIN or CVV. No bank or government office will ask for them.
- Cyber fraud: call **1930** and report at **cybercrime.gov.in** immediately, and inform your bank.
- Save in regulated banks, post offices or cooperative banks. Bank deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank by DICGC.
