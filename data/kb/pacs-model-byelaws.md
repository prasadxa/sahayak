# Model Bye-laws for Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS)

This note explains the Model Bye-laws for PACS in simple words: what they are, what activities they allow, and what they mean for members.

## What is a PACS?

A Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS) is a village-level cooperative society owned by its farmer members. Traditionally a PACS gives short-term crop loans and supplies farm inputs such as seeds and fertiliser. PACS are the base of the cooperative credit system: PACS are linked to District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs), which are linked to State Cooperative Banks.

## What are bye-laws?

Bye-laws are the written rules of a cooperative society. They say who can become a member, what the society can do, how the managing committee (board) is formed and elected, how meetings are held, how profits are used, and how disputes are handled. Bye-laws must follow the state's Cooperative Societies Act and are registered with the Registrar of Cooperative Societies.

## What are the Model Bye-laws for PACS?

In 2023 the **Ministry of Cooperation**, Government of India, prepared **Model Bye-laws for PACS** in consultation with states, NABARD and other stakeholders, and shared them with all states and union territories. Because cooperatives are a state subject, each state adopts the model bye-laws through its own process, sometimes with changes. So the exact bye-laws that apply to your PACS depend on your state and on what your PACS has adopted.

The main aim of the model bye-laws is to turn PACS into **multipurpose** village businesses, so they earn from more than just lending, become financially stronger, and serve farmers better.

## What activities can a PACS take up under the model bye-laws?

The model bye-laws allow a PACS to take up **more than 25 business activities**. Examples include:

- short-term and medium-term loans to members (for example through the Kisan Credit Card);
- supply of seeds, fertilisers and pesticides;
- **dairy** and milk collection;
- **fisheries** activities;
- floriculture, beekeeping and other allied activities;
- **godowns** and warehousing, and procurement of foodgrains;
- custom hiring centres for farm machinery;
- food processing units;
- Fair Price Shops under the public distribution system;
- **LPG distributorship** and **petrol/diesel retail outlets**;
- **Common Service Centres (CSC)**, which give villagers access to many government and digital services;
- **Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Jan Aushadhi Kendras**, which sell quality generic medicines at low prices;
- **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendras (PMKSK)**, which provide fertiliser, seeds, soil testing and farm advice in one place;
- community irrigation and water-related services.

Which of these a particular PACS actually runs depends on its members' decision, its finances, approvals from the concerned departments, and the state's rules.

## How do I become a member of a PACS?

The general steps are:

1. Check that you are eligible. Usually you must be an adult living in, or owning or cultivating land in, the PACS's area of operation, and you must meet the other conditions in the bye-laws.
2. Get the membership application form from the PACS office.
3. Fill it in and attach the documents asked for, such as identity proof (for example Aadhaar), address proof, a photograph, and land records if required.
4. Pay the admission fee and buy at least the minimum number of shares.
5. The managing committee considers the application. If it is accepted, you are entered in the register of members.

The share value, admission fee and exact eligibility rules are set in the bye-laws and differ from state to state and society to society. Ask the PACS secretary for the current figures.

## Can women, landless labourers and small farmers join?

The model bye-laws aim to make PACS membership broad and inclusive, including women and weaker sections, and they provide for representation of women and of Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes on the managing committee. The exact eligibility rules for tenant farmers, landless labourers and others depend on the bye-laws your PACS has adopted.

## What are my rights as a PACS member?

As a member you generally have the right to:

- attend the general body meeting (including the annual general meeting) and vote;
- stand for election to the managing committee, if you meet the conditions;
- use the services of the society, such as loans and input supply;
- see the society's bye-laws, audited accounts and annual report;
- share in the dividend, if the general body declares one;
- raise complaints and get them looked into.

## What are my duties as a member?

Members are expected to follow the bye-laws, repay loans on time, attend general body meetings, take part in the society's business, and keep their details (such as address and nominee) up to date with the society.

## How is a PACS run?

The **general body** of all members is the highest authority. It elects a **managing committee** (board) for a fixed term, as set by the state law. The committee appoints a **secretary** or chief executive who manages daily work. The accounts must be audited every year and placed before the general body. The model bye-laws put stress on transparency, regular elections, professional management and the use of computers for accounts.

## Can a member be removed?

A member can be removed (expelled) only for reasons and through the procedure given in the bye-laws and the state Act, and usually only after being given a chance to explain. If you think you were removed unfairly, you can appeal to the Registrar of Cooperative Societies or follow the dispute process in your state's law.

## How do the model bye-laws help farmers?

When a PACS runs more businesses, farmers can get more services in their own village: loans, inputs, storage, machinery, medicines, fuel and digital services. A stronger PACS can also give better prices and pay dividends to members.

## Key points to remember

- The Model Bye-laws for PACS were circulated by the Ministry of Cooperation in 2023.
- They let PACS take up 25+ activities, including dairy, fisheries, godowns, LPG and petrol outlets, CSCs, Jan Aushadhi Kendras and PM Kisan Samriddhi Kendras.
- Each state adopts them in its own way, so always check the bye-laws of your own PACS.

Verify the latest details at: https://www.cooperation.gov.in/ and the website of your state's Registrar of Cooperative Societies
