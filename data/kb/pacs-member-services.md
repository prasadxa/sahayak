# PACS Member Services: A Practical Guide

This note explains what a Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS, also called a "sahakari samiti" or village cooperative society) does, how to become a member, and which services members can use.

## What is a PACS?

A **Primary Agricultural Credit Society (PACS)** is a cooperative society at the village or panchayat level, owned and controlled by its farmer members. It is the first level of the short-term cooperative credit system:

- **PACS** at the village level lends to farmers;
- the **District Central Cooperative Bank (DCCB)** at the district level lends to PACS;
- the **State Cooperative Bank (StCB)** at the state level supports the DCCBs.

There are about one lakh PACS in India, reaching a very large number of villages.

## What services does a PACS give its members?

The services depend on your PACS, but can include:

- **Crop loans**, often through the **Kisan Credit Card (KCC)**, and other short-term and medium-term loans;
- **Farm inputs**, such as seeds, fertilisers and pesticides, at fair prices;
- **Procurement** of crops, and **godowns** for storing grain;
- **Custom hiring centres**, where members can rent tractors and other farm machinery;
- **Fair Price Shops** under the public distribution system;
- **Common Service Centre (CSC)** services, such as online applications, certificates and bill payments;
- **Jan Aushadhi Kendras** selling low-cost generic medicines;
- **PM Kisan Samriddhi Kendras**, which sell fertiliser and offer soil testing and farm advice;
- **LPG distribution** or **petrol/diesel outlets**;
- enrolment for crop insurance under **PMFBY**.

Under the **Model Bye-laws for PACS** (2023), a PACS can take up more than 25 such activities. Ask your PACS which services it offers today.

## How do I become a member of a PACS?

1. Visit your PACS office and ask for the **membership form**.
2. Check that you meet the eligibility rules in the bye-laws. Usually you must be an adult who lives in, or farms land in, the area of the PACS.
3. Attach documents such as **Aadhaar**, a photograph, address proof and **land records** or a tenancy or cultivation certificate, as asked.
4. Pay the **admission fee** and buy the minimum **share(s)**.
5. After the managing committee approves, you become a member. Ask for a **share certificate** or membership card and your membership number.

The fee, share value and exact rules differ by state and society, so ask the PACS secretary.

## How do I get a crop loan from my PACS?

1. Become a member of the PACS.
2. Apply with your land records or cultivation details and the crops you plan to grow.
3. The PACS works out your loan limit using the **scale of finance** for your crop and district.
4. After approval, the loan is given in cash or kind (for example fertiliser), or through your KCC, as per the rules.
5. **Repay on time.** Under the Modified Interest Subvention Scheme, eligible short-term crop loans up to ₹3 lakh carry 7% interest, and prompt repayment gives an extra 3% incentive, so the effective rate is 4%. Check the current limits and how your PACS applies them.

## What are my rights as a member?

- Attend the **general body meeting** and **vote**.
- **Stand for election** to the managing committee, if eligible.
- Get **services** of the society on fair and equal terms.
- See the **bye-laws**, the **audited accounts** and the **annual report**.
- Get a **passbook or statement** of your loan and share account, and **receipts** for every payment.
- Get a share in the **dividend**, if declared by the general body.
- **Complain** if something is wrong, first to the society and then to the Registrar.

## What are my responsibilities as a member?

- Follow the bye-laws and the decisions of the general body.
- **Repay loans on time**, so the society can lend to others.
- Attend meetings and take part in decisions.
- Keep your **nominee**, address and mobile number up to date with the society.

## How are PACS elections held?

The members elect the **managing committee** (board) for a fixed term set by your state's cooperative law. In many states, elections are conducted by a state cooperative election authority or commission. The bye-laws and state law provide for seats reserved for women and for weaker sections. Ask the Registrar's office or your state's cooperative election authority for the election schedule.

## What is PACS computerisation and how does it help me?

Under the Ministry of Cooperation's project to **computerise functional PACS**, PACS are moving their accounts and business to a common computer software linked with the DCCB and State Cooperative Bank. For members, this means:

- faster loan processing;
- fewer mistakes in accounts;
- clearer and more transparent records;
- easier audits.

## How can I check my loan or share account?

Ask the PACS for an updated **passbook** or **statement**. Check every entry: date, amount, interest and repayment. If you find a mistake, write to the secretary at once and keep a copy of your letter.

## Can a PACS help with government schemes?

Yes. Many PACS work as **Common Service Centres**, where you can apply for government schemes and services. PACS may also help with **PMFBY crop insurance** enrolment and farm-input subsidies. Never pay anyone extra to "speed up" a scheme application, and never share your OTP or PIN.

## Whom do I contact if my PACS does not help?

1. Write to the PACS **secretary** and **managing committee**.
2. Then go to the **Assistant** or **Deputy Registrar of Cooperative Societies** for your area.
3. Then go to the **Registrar of Cooperative Societies** of your state.

You can also file a grievance through **Sahayak** and track it with your GRV reference number. See the Sahayak note on grievance redressal.

Verify the latest details at: https://www.cooperation.gov.in/ , https://www.nabard.org/ and your state Registrar of Cooperative Societies' website
