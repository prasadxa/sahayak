# Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002 and the 2023 Amendment

This note explains, in simple words, the main law for cooperative societies that work in more than one state, and the changes made to it in 2023.

## What is a multi-state cooperative society?

A multi-state cooperative society is a cooperative whose members and area of work are spread over more than one state. Large dairy federations, cooperative banks with branches in several states, and national-level cooperatives such as marketing and fertiliser federations are common examples.

A cooperative that works only inside one state is a state cooperative society. It is registered under that state's own Cooperative Societies Act, and the state's Registrar of Cooperative Societies supervises it. Most village cooperatives, including Primary Agricultural Credit Societies (PACS), are state cooperative societies.

## Which law governs multi-state cooperative societies?

Multi-state cooperatives are governed by the **Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002** (often called the MSCS Act) and the rules made under it. The Act covers how a society is registered, how its members, general body and board work, how elections, audit and accounts are handled, how disputes are settled, and how a society can be merged, divided or wound up.

## Who registers and supervises multi-state cooperatives?

The **Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies (CRCS)** registers and supervises multi-state cooperative societies. The CRCS office works under the **Ministry of Cooperation**, Government of India, which was formed in **July 2021**. The CRCS runs an online portal at crcs.gov.in for services such as registration, amendment of bye-laws, filing returns and complaints.

## Is forming a cooperative a right?

Yes. The 97th Constitutional Amendment (2011) added the right to form cooperative societies to the fundamental freedoms in the Constitution of India. The Constitution also asks the State to promote the voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, democratic control and professional management of cooperatives.

## What changed with the Multi-State Co-operative Societies (Amendment) Act, 2023?

Parliament passed the Multi-State Co-operative Societies (Amendment) Act in 2023 to improve governance, transparency and accountability in multi-state cooperatives and to make elections more regular. The main changes are explained below.

### What is the Co-operative Election Authority?

The amendment created a **Co-operative Election Authority**. It conducts the elections to the boards of multi-state cooperative societies, so that elections happen on time and in a free and fair way, instead of each society's outgoing board running its own election.

### What is the Co-operative Ombudsman?

The amendment provides for a **Co-operative Ombudsman**. A member of a multi-state cooperative society who has a complaint, for example about their deposits, the services of the society, or their rights as a member, can approach the Ombudsman. It usually helps to raise the problem with the society in writing first. The Ombudsman looks into the complaint and can give directions. This gives members a way to get a grievance heard without going straight to court.

### What is the Co-operative Information Officer?

Every multi-state cooperative society must appoint a **Co-operative Information Officer**. Members can ask this officer for information about the affairs and management of the society, such as accounts and decisions. This is meant to make societies more open with their own members.

### Are there seats reserved on the board?

Yes. The amendment reserves seats on the board of a multi-state cooperative society for **women** and for members from the **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes**, so that these groups have a voice in how the society is run. The exact number of reserved seats is set out in the Act, so check the current text of the Act or ask the CRCS office.

### What is the rehabilitation and development fund?

The amendment set up a **Co-operative Rehabilitation, Reconstruction and Development Fund**. It is used to revive sick multi-state cooperative societies. Financially sound multi-state societies contribute to this fund according to the rules. The contribution rules and limits are prescribed under the Act and rules, so check them at the official source.

### What other changes did the amendment make?

The amendment also includes provisions on:

- stronger audit and accounts rules, so that members and the government get a clearer picture of a society's finances;
- rules against board members taking part in decisions where they or their relatives have an interest;
- clearer rules for merging a state cooperative society into a multi-state society;
- more use of digital processes through the CRCS.

The details of each of these, including thresholds and timelines, are in the Act and its rules. Sahayak does not quote section numbers or amounts from memory. Please read the official text for exact wording.

## Does the MSCS Act apply to my village PACS?

Usually not. A village PACS normally works inside one state, so it is governed by your **state's Cooperative Societies Act** and the state's model bye-laws. The rules on elections, audit, membership and complaints for state societies are set by each state, and they differ from state to state. For a state society, the right office is usually the Assistant Registrar, Deputy Registrar or Registrar of Cooperative Societies of your district or state.

## How can I check whether a society is multi-state?

You can:

1. look at the society's registration certificate or bye-laws, which say which law it is registered under;
2. search the list of registered multi-state cooperative societies on the CRCS portal; or
3. ask the society's office or the district cooperative office.

## Where can a member of a multi-state society complain?

First, raise the issue in writing with the society's management and keep a copy. If it is not solved, you can approach the Co-operative Ombudsman or use the complaint facility on the CRCS portal. Complaints against central government offices, including the Ministry of Cooperation, can also be filed on CPGRAMS at pgportal.gov.in. See the Sahayak note on grievance redressal for step-by-step guidance.

## Key points to remember

- Multi-state cooperatives: MSCS Act 2002, as amended in 2023, supervised by the Central Registrar (CRCS).
- State cooperatives, including most PACS: your state's Cooperative Societies Act and the state Registrar.
- The 2023 amendment added a Co-operative Election Authority, a Co-operative Ombudsman, Co-operative Information Officers, reserved board seats for women and SC/ST members, and a rehabilitation and development fund.
