# Ministry of Cooperation: Schemes and Initiatives

This note explains the main schemes and initiatives of the Ministry of Cooperation, Government of India, in simple words, and how members of cooperatives can benefit.

## When was the Ministry of Cooperation formed and why?

The **Ministry of Cooperation** was formed in **July 2021**. Before that, cooperation was handled by the Ministry of Agriculture. A separate ministry was created to give focus to the cooperative movement and to strengthen cooperatives at the grassroots. Its guiding idea is **"Sahakar se Samriddhi"**, which means "prosperity through cooperation".

## What is the scheme for computerisation of PACS?

Under the **Computerisation of PACS** project, functional Primary Agricultural Credit Societies across the country are being computerised on a common, cloud-based software. The PACS are linked with their District Central Cooperative Banks and State Cooperative Banks, and NABARD is the implementing agency.

What it means for members:

- loan accounts and other records are kept on computer, which reduces mistakes and delays;
- accounts and transactions become more transparent;
- the PACS can take up more businesses, such as input supply, procurement and storage, using the same software;
- the software is available in local languages.

## What is the "world's largest grain storage plan in the cooperative sector"?

The government approved a plan described as the **world's largest grain storage plan in the cooperative sector**. Under it, PACS can set up agriculture infrastructure such as **godowns (warehouses)**, custom hiring centres, processing units and fair price shops at the village level. The plan works by bringing together (converging) existing government schemes, such as the Agriculture Infrastructure Fund and schemes for agricultural marketing and farm mechanisation.

What it means for farmers:

- storage close to the village, so crops need not be sold in distress right after harvest;
- less loss of grain during storage and transport;
- more income for the PACS, which helps members.

It is being rolled out in phases, starting with pilot projects. Ask your PACS or district cooperative office whether a godown is planned in your area.

## What is the plan for 2 lakh new cooperatives?

The government approved a plan to set up **2 lakh new multipurpose PACS, dairy cooperatives and fishery cooperatives** over **five years**, especially in panchayats and villages that do not yet have one. This is done by bringing together schemes of different departments, with support from NABARD, the National Dairy Development Board (NDDB) and the National Fisheries Development Board (NFDB).

If your village has no PACS, dairy or fishery cooperative, you can ask the district cooperative office how a new one can be formed.

## What are the new national cooperative societies NCEL, NCOL and BBSSL?

Three new national-level multi-state cooperative societies were set up in 2023:

- **National Cooperative Export Limited (NCEL):** helps cooperatives export their products, so members can get better prices in foreign markets.
- **National Cooperative Organics Limited (NCOL):** helps cooperatives produce, certify, brand and market **organic** products.
- **Bharatiya Beej Sahakari Samiti Limited (BBSSL):** works on producing, processing and distributing good quality **certified seeds**, including traditional varieties, through cooperatives.

Cooperatives, including PACS, can become members of these societies. Ask your PACS or district cooperative office how to connect.

## What are the Model Bye-laws for PACS?

In 2023 the Ministry circulated **Model Bye-laws for PACS**, which let PACS take up **more than 25 business activities**, such as dairy, fisheries, godowns, LPG and petrol outlets, and more. States adopt them through their own process. See the Sahayak note "Model Bye-laws for PACS" for details.

## Which services can PACS now offer in villages?

Under various initiatives of the Ministry, many PACS now also work as:

- **Common Service Centres (CSC):** give villagers access to many government and digital services, such as applications, bill payments and certificates, close to home;
- **Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Jan Aushadhi Kendras:** sell quality generic medicines at lower prices;
- **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samriddhi Kendras (PMKSK):** one-stop shops for fertiliser, seeds, soil testing and farm advice;
- **LPG distributors** and **petrol/diesel retail outlets**;
- **Farmer Producer Organisations (FPOs)**, so small farmers can buy and sell together.

Not every PACS offers every service. It depends on approvals, finances and member decisions.

## What is the National Cooperative Database?

The Ministry built a **National Cooperative Database** that records information about cooperative societies across the country, such as their sector and location. It helps the government plan schemes and find villages without cooperatives.

## Which other bodies help cooperatives?

- **NABARD** supports rural credit cooperatives and is the implementing agency for PACS computerisation.
- The **National Cooperative Development Corporation (NCDC)** gives loans and grants for cooperative projects such as processing, storage and marketing.
- The **Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies (CRCS)** registers and supervises multi-state cooperative societies.

## How can I benefit from these schemes?

1. Become a member of your local PACS, dairy or fishery cooperative.
2. Attend general body meetings and ask what new services the society is planning.
3. Ask your PACS secretary or the district cooperative office (Assistant or Deputy Registrar of Cooperative Societies) about schemes in your area.
4. Check the Ministry of Cooperation website for the latest guidelines.
