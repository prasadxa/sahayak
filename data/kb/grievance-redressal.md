# How Cooperative Members Can Get Their Grievances Resolved

This note explains, step by step, where a member of a cooperative society (such as a PACS, dairy cooperative or cooperative bank) can complain, and how to write a good complaint.

## What kinds of problems can I complain about?

Common grievances include:

- being refused membership, or being removed without a fair process;
- delays or refusal of loans or other services you are entitled to;
- wrong entries in your loan or share account, or not getting a passbook or receipt;
- elections not being held on time, or unfair elections;
- the society not following its bye-laws, or not holding general body meetings;
- not being shown the accounts, audit report or bye-laws;
- suspected misuse of money or fraud by office-bearers or staff;
- not getting the benefit of a government scheme through the society.

## Step 1: How do I complain to the society first?

Most problems are best solved first at the society level.

1. **Write to the secretary and the managing committee** (board) of your society. Clearly explain the problem.
2. **Get an acknowledgement.** Ask for a receipt with the date, or send it by registered post, and keep a copy.
3. **Raise it at the general body meeting**, where all members can discuss it.
4. Give the society a reasonable time to reply.

## Step 2: Where do I go if my state cooperative society does not act?

Most village cooperatives, including PACS, are **state cooperative societies**, registered under the state's Cooperative Societies Act. If the society does not solve your problem:

1. Complain to the **Assistant Registrar** or **Deputy Registrar of Cooperative Societies** for your taluka or district. This office supervises the society.
2. If needed, take it to the **Registrar of Cooperative Societies** of your state.
3. For disputes between members and the society, many states' laws provide for **arbitration**, a **cooperative court** or a **cooperative tribunal**. The exact process and time limits depend on your state's law.

Many states also have an online grievance portal or a CM helpline. Check your state government's website.

## Step 3: Where do I go if it is a multi-state cooperative society?

If your society works in more than one state, it is registered under the **Multi-State Co-operative Societies Act, 2002**. Then:

1. Complain to the society first, as in Step 1.
2. Approach the **Co-operative Ombudsman**, set up under the 2023 amendment to the Act, which hears complaints of members of multi-state cooperatives.
3. Use the **Central Registrar of Cooperative Societies (CRCS)** portal at **crcs.gov.in**, which supervises multi-state societies.
4. For information about the society's affairs, you can ask its **Co-operative Information Officer**.
5. Complaints about board elections of multi-state societies are handled under the **Co-operative Election Authority**.

## Step 4: How do I use CPGRAMS?

**CPGRAMS** (Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System) at **pgportal.gov.in** is the Government of India's online portal for grievances against central ministries and departments, including the **Ministry of Cooperation**.

1. Register on pgportal.gov.in with your mobile number.
2. Choose the ministry or department and describe your grievance.
3. Upload supporting documents if you have them.
4. Note your **registration number** and use it to track the status.

CPGRAMS is best for problems with government offices and schemes. Disputes inside a state society are usually handled by the state Registrar first.

## How can I file a grievance through Sahayak?

You can ask Sahayak to file a grievance for you in your own language. Sahayak will ask for the category, a short subject, a description and, optionally, your contact details, district and society name. After filing, you get a reference number that looks like **GRV-XXXXXXXX**. Keep it safe, and use the **Track** page with this number to see status updates from officers.

Filing through Sahayak records your grievance for the officers using this system. For a formal legal dispute, you may still need to approach the Registrar, the Ombudsman or the court as described above.

## What should my complaint include?

A clear complaint is resolved faster. Include:

- your **name**, **membership number** (if any), village and **district**;
- the **name of the society** and its registration number if you know it;
- **what happened**, with **dates**, amounts and names of people involved;
- what you have already done (for example, "wrote to the secretary on …");
- copies of documents such as receipts, passbook pages, notices or letters;
- **what you want**: for example, a corrected account entry, a loan decision, or a copy of the audit report.

Write politely and stick to facts. Keep copies of everything you send and receive.

## What if the problem is fraud or cheating?

- For **online or banking fraud**, call **1930** immediately and report at **cybercrime.gov.in**, and inform your bank.
- For **misuse of society funds**, complain in writing to the Registrar of Cooperative Societies, who can order an inquiry or inspection.
- For criminal cheating, you can also file a complaint at the **police station**.

## What if my complaint is about a bank?

Complain to the bank first. If the bank does not reply within 30 days or you are not satisfied, you can approach the **RBI Ombudsman** through **cms.rbi.org.in**, depending on the type of bank.

## Key points to remember

- Start with the society's managing committee, in writing, and keep proof.
- State societies (most PACS): Assistant or Deputy Registrar, then the state Registrar of Cooperative Societies.
- Multi-state societies: the Co-operative Ombudsman and the CRCS portal (crcs.gov.in).
- Central government offices and schemes: CPGRAMS (pgportal.gov.in).
- Time limits and procedures differ by state and by law, so confirm them with the concerned office.

Verify the latest details at: https://pgportal.gov.in/ , https://crcs.gov.in/ and your state Registrar of Cooperative Societies' website
